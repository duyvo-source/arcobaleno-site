What a full audit looks like

The whole report, start to finish, so you can see what you are buying before you write to us. The strategy, the client and every figure in it are made up.

SPECIMEN, NOT CLIENT WORK. No client, no engagement, no real data, no real firm. We built a fictional order-book signal and audited it against all eight checks, so the format is real and the findings are not. For defects we actually found, in our own research, see the ten findings on the front page. Order-book data like this specimen's is quoted separately from the tier prices, because of its size.

BACKTEST FORENSICS — FULL AUDIT
Prepared for [client redacted — specimen]
Strategy obi-signal-v4 — order-book-imbalance directional signal, 12 crypto perpetual pairs
What it emits a three-way call — buy / sell / flat — every 15 minutes per pair
Claim under test 61.4% directional hit rate · +38.0% annualised on a 1× notional overlay · Sharpe 1.62 · max drawdown −11.2%, 2023-01-01 → 2025-12-31
Scope Full audit. All eight checks, FC-1 through FC-8.
Material examined signal generator, backtest engine, scoring script, 3 years of 1-second L2 snapshots (5 levels a side), exported OHLCV used to score the calls, and the two documents stating the claim
Method every finding ships with a script the client runs themselves

Verdict

The signal is real. The number is not.

Those are two different findings and both matter, so neither is buried.

What holds. obi-signal-v4 carries genuine directional information. On six months of data that was never used to choose anything, it calls direction correctly 53.1% of the time against a 33.3% chance floor on 1,180 scored calls — a margin that is not remotely explicable by luck (p < 0.001). Nothing in this report says the model is noise, and we want that read before anything else, because most of what follows is subtraction.

What does not hold. The +38.0% annualised figure does not survive contact with the checklist. Corrected for six defects — none of them in the model, all of them in what the backtest assumed about execution and about the clock — the same signal on the same data returns +2.2% annualised, with a 95% confidence interval of −5.4% to +9.9%.

That interval includes zero. The honest statement of the claim is not "this makes 38% a year." It is: "this predicts direction better than chance, by a margin that the current execution assumptions are too optimistic to convert into a demonstrated return."

And the failure is not spread evenly — it is concentrated exactly where the money is. The single largest correction (§4, FC-7) comes from bars in the top 1% of realised volatility. Those bars carry 34% of the claimed P&L and are the bars where the strategy's fill assumption is least defensible. The strategy looks best precisely where it is least demonstrable.

On the known live miss. The client reported one live event where the signal called buy and the correct call was sell. The audit's job was to say whether that was one event or a class. It is a class — see §5. Fourteen events in the sample share its structure; nine of the fourteen invert when the signal is recomputed at the latency it actually runs at. The miss was not bad luck. It is what this signal does in that regime, and it will do it again.

1. What we were asked

"61.4% hit rate, +38% annualised, Sharpe 1.62, max DD −11.2%. Order-book imbalance, 12 perp pairs, 15-minute calls, 2023 through 2025."

The figure appears in a strategy memo and in a one-page tear sheet prepared for investors. The two agree, which is worth noting: agreement between two documents is not corroboration when both are downstream of one script. They were.

What the client told us before we started (our intake form asks "anything you already suspect is wrong?"): one live event, roughly a year before the engagement, where the signal called buy into what should have been a sell. The client's own read was that it was a one-off. That answer shaped the audit — it is why FC-5 and FC-7 were worked before FC-3, against the usual cheap-and-structural-first order. It was the right call; see §5.

2. FC-1 — Does it repeat? PASS

Method. fctools/fc1_reproduce.py --cmd "<their command>": 20 runs in an identical environment, plus runs under three different PYTHONHASHSEED values, on two machines with different CPU counts.

Finding. It reproduces. Maximum spread across 20 runs: 0.31pp of annualised return, attributable to a documented floating-point reduction order in the rolling imbalance window. Hit rate was bit-identical in every run.

This is a clean result and it is worth what it costs. Most of the strategies that reach us fail here (case study 1 exists because one of ours did). A signal that returns the same number on a machine that has never seen it is a strategy whose author can be taken at their word about everything downstream, and it meant the remaining seven checks were testing the method rather than chasing an unstable number.

What passing does not mean. It means the code is deterministic. It says nothing about whether the number is right — the next six sections are all defects in a perfectly reproducible pipeline.

THE FIX — nothing to fix, one thing to keep. Pin PYTHONHASHSEED and the reduction order in CI so this stays true as the code changes. Reproducibility is not a property you have; it is one you keep. Effort: under an hour, once.

Reproduce: python3 fctools/fc1_reproduce.py --cmd "python3 run_backtest.py --config prod.yaml" --runs 20

3. FC-6 — Was it cherry-picked? The largest single correction

What we asked. How many versions were tried, and was this one chosen before or after its performance was visible?

What we found. The repository contains 41 distinct threshold configurations in configs/, dated across fourteen months, plus a sweep script that evaluates all of them on the full 2023–2025 period. The shipped configuration is the argmax of that sweep.

There was no holdout. Not a weak one — none. Every configuration was scored on every day of data, and the best was kept.

Why this is not an accusation. Nobody did anything dishonest. This is the most common finding we make, it is made against our own research routinely (case study 8), and the author's instinct — "try the variants, keep the one that works" — is correct in every field except this one. The problem is arithmetic, not integrity: with 41 tries on one sample, the best result is the sample's luckiest result, and it will not repeat.

Quantifying it. We carved the final six months (2025-07-01 → 2025-12-31) and confirmed against the client's own commit history that no configuration file was modified after 2025-06-14 — so that window is genuinely untouched by selection. Re-reading the shipped, unmodified configuration on it:

full sample (selected on) untouched holdout
scored calls 7,104 1,180
directional hit rate 61.4% 53.1%
annualised (pre-other-corrections) +38.0% +21.9%
rank of shipped config among the 41 1st 17th

That last row is the finding. On data it was not chosen against, the shipped configuration is ordinary — 17th of 41, indistinguishable from the middle of its own family. The spread between 1st and 17th on the selection sample was a spread between lucky draws, not between better and worse models.

And the part that survives. 53.1% against a 33.3% floor on 1,180 independent three-way calls is p < 0.001. Selection inflated the number. It did not manufacture it. There is something here.

THE FIX — this one cannot be fixed backwards, only forwards. There is no way to un-see 41 results, and no re-analysis recovers what selection consumed. Going forward, two changes: (a) cut a holdout before the next parameter change and do not read it until the change is final; (b) for anything you intend to show an investor, fix the bar in writing before the window opens. Effort: a policy decision plus a day of tooling. Note that the 17th-of-41 rank is not a reason to switch configurations — the ranking on the holdout is itself one draw.

Reproduce: python3 audit/fc6_holdout.py --configs configs/ --holdout 2025-07-01:2025-12-31 --freeze-check .git

4. FC-7 — Could you have traded it? The largest correction in the return

What we asked. Could every position in the result actually have been bought and sold, at the price used, at that size?

What we found. The engine fills every call as a resting limit order at the mid price prevailing at signal time, and treats the fill as certain. That assumption is doing an enormous amount of work, and it is doing the most work exactly where it is least true.

The concentration, first — because it reframes everything. We measured where the claimed P&L actually comes from:

bars, ranked by realised 15-minute volatility share of bars share of claimed P&L
top 1% 0.8% 34.1%
top 5% 4.9% 61.3%
remaining 95% 95.1% 38.7%

A third of the return is produced on eight-tenths of one percent of the bars. Whatever is true about the fill assumption on those bars is very nearly the whole result.

What is true about them. A resting limit order fills when someone crosses the spread to hit it. On a fast move, the orders that fill are the ones on the wrong side — you are filled because the market is leaving. We measured, on each of those top-1% bars, whether the modelled fill price was still available:
top-1% bars where the mid moved past the limit price within 1 second 71.4%
median adverse move before the order could have rested 19 bps
modelled fills at a price that was never touched again in the bar 2,847 of 7,104 calls

The correction. We re-ran the engine with a single change — on any bar where the book moved away before the order could rest, the position is taken as a taker, at the price that actually existed, instead of as a maker at the price that did not. The client's own venue data supplies both. Everything else is untouched.

+26.1% → +8.2% annualised. A 17.9 percentage-point correction, and the single largest in this report.

Why this is a defect and not a difference of opinion. The engine is not modelling an optimistic fill. It is modelling a fill conditional on information it did not have — whether the order would fill is decided by what happens after the signal, and the engine resolves that question by assuming the favourable answer every time. That is look-ahead in the fill logic, and it is the defect FC-7 exists to find.

THE FIX — invert the default. Model every fill as a taker unless there is evidence in the book that a resting order would have filled, rather than the reverse. This is one function in the engine and it makes the assumption conservative instead of optimistic. Effort: 2–3 days including re-running the sweep. How to know it worked: at a position cap high enough that the constraint never binds, the corrected engine and the current one must agree bit-for-bit — if they do not, the change altered something it should not have.

Reproduce: python3 audit/fc7_fill_realism.py --book l2/ --calls signals.parquet --mode counterfactual Per-call output in fc7_fill_audit.csv — 7,104 rows, one per call, not a summary.

5. FC-5 — Is the data current? The finding that explains the known miss

What we asked. The signal reads a book snapshot. How old is that snapshot at the moment the decision is made?

What we found. The backtest treats the snapshot as instantaneous — the book state at time t produces the call at time t. In production it cannot be. We instrumented the live path from the client's own logs:

book snapshot age at decision time
median, all conditions 240 ms
p99, all conditions 1.9 s
p99, during top-1% volatility bars 6.2 s
maximum observed 11.4 s

The age is not constant — it is worst exactly when the market is fastest. Snapshot latency rises with message rate, and message rate rises with volatility. The backtest models a book that is zero seconds old; the live system, on the bars that carry a third of the P&L, is reading a book that is a median of 4.4 seconds stale.

Four seconds is a long time in a book that is being lifted. The resting bid depth the signal reads as support has, by the time the call is emitted, frequently already been taken or pulled. The signal sees a wall that is no longer there and reads it as strength.

This is the mechanism behind the event the client already knew about. We did not go looking for it to explain the miss — it came out of the latency instrumentation — but it fits exactly: a buy call, into a move that had already happened, because the book that justified it was seconds old.

Is it one event or a class? This was the question the client asked us to answer. We identified every event in the three-year sample sharing its structure — a top-1% volatility bar preceded by a ≥40% reduction in same-side resting depth within the snapshot latency window:
events of this structure, 2023–2025 14
calls that invert when recomputed at measured latency 9 of 14
of those 9, calls that were scored as hits in the backtest 9 of 9
combined contribution of those 9 to claimed P&L 11.4pp of the 38.0%

A class, not an accident. Nine of the fourteen largest events in the sample are scored as wins by a backtest reading a book the live system does not have. The reported live miss is the fourteen-event population behaving normally, and it will recur on the next event of that structure.

The correction. Recomputing every call on a book aged to the measured latency distribution for its volatility regime: +8.2% → +4.1% annualised.

THE FIX — three things, and the cheap one matters most. (a) A live staleness guard: suppress any call computed on a book older than a stated threshold. This is roughly a day of work and it is the change that stops the next event of this class reaching the market. It costs you some calls; on this sample it would have suppressed 9 of the 14 and none of them were calls you wanted. (b) Feed the measured age distribution into the backtest, so research and production agree about what the signal can see. (c) Reduce the latency itself — the largest project, and the only one that recovers the suppressed calls rather than discarding them. Effort: (a) ~1 day · (b) ~2 days · (c) an infrastructure project.

Reproduce: python3 audit/fc5_snapshot_age.py --logs prod_logs/ --replay --age-model measured The 14 events are enumerated with timestamps in fc5_regime_events.csv.

6. FC-3 — Are the costs real?

What we asked. Where did the fee figure come from, and is it charged correctly?

What we found. The engine applies a maker rebate to 100% of fills. The client's own venue statements, for the live period, show a realised maker ratio of 61%.

Two errors compound here:

  1. The mix is wrong. 39% of fills are takers paying a fee, modelled as makers receiving a rebate. The per-fill error is the full rebate-to-fee spread, not the difference between two fees.
  2. It is wrong in a correlated way, not a random way. The taker fills are not scattered uniformly — they concentrate on exactly the fast bars from §4 and §5, because that is when a resting order does not fill and the system crosses the spread. The cost error is largest on the bars carrying the most P&L. Correcting the mix to a flat 61/39 average would understate the damage; we corrected it bar by bar.
modelled measured
maker share of fills 100% 61%
maker share on top-1% volatility bars 100% 23%
effective round-trip cost, all bars −1.0 bp (rebate) +4.4 bp
effective round-trip cost, top-1% bars −1.0 bp (rebate) +9.1 bp

The correction: +36.2% → +29.6% annualised, a 6.6pp reduction.

Note the ordering effect. Because this correction and §4's are both concentrated on the same bars, applying them in a different order moves the intermediate figures. The final corrected number is order-independent; the waterfall's middle rows are not, and we state the order used at the top of it rather than presenting the intermediates as standalone facts.

THE FIX — read the ratio, don't assume it. Apply the realised maker/taker split from the venue statements per bar, not a constant. The statements already exist and are already reconciled; this is a lookup, not a model. Effort: half a day. Expect the corrected cost line to keep moving as the fill model in FC-7 changes — these two are coupled, so measure them together and re-read the cost number after the fill change lands, not before.

Reproduce: python3 audit/fc3_cost_reconcile.py --statements venue_statements/ --fills fills.parquet

7. FC-4 — Is the data clean? Two correct datasets, one wrong join

This is the finding that most surprised the client, so it is written out at length.

What we asked. Was every row checked? Can one source poison the result?

What we found. Nothing is wrong with either dataset. The defect is that there are two of them, and they are not the same market.

  • The signal is computed from L2 order-book snapshots taken from the execution venue — the perpetual contract actually traded.
  • The calls are scored against an OHLCV series exported from a charting platform, which for these symbols serves an aggregated cross-venue index, not the traded contract.

Both series are accurate. The charting export is not corrupted, the prints are real, the timestamps are honest. It is simply a different price series than the one a fill would have occurred at, and it is being used to decide whether each call was right.

Why "the data is correct" does not settle this. Correctness of a price series is a statement about its values. Using it to score a trade makes two further claims that correctness does not support: that it is the price you could have transacted at, and that its timestamp means the same thing as your decision timestamp. Neither holds here.

index vs traded-venue basis
median absolute basis 0.8 bp
p99 absolute basis 11 bps
p99 during top-1% volatility bars 41 bps
bar-close timestamp convention index bars stamped at open, engine reads them as close

The timestamp convention is the more serious half. A 15-minute bar stamped at its open, read as though stamped at its close, hands the scorer a 15-minute look-ahead on every call. The basis costs accuracy; the convention costs the integrity of the scoring.

The correction. Rescoring every call against the traded venue's own trades, with the timestamp convention corrected:
scored hits that flip to misses 7.1%
scored misses that flip to hits 1.4%
net effect on hit rate −5.7pp

+29.6% → +26.1% annualised.

A single bad day, checked separately. Per FC-4's second question, we checked whether any one window carries disproportionate weight. One venue outage (2024-03-11, 71 minutes) left the book frozen; the imbalance ratio on a frozen book is constant, which the signal read as a sustained extreme and converted into 14 calls, 12 of them scored as hits. Removing the outage window costs 0.4pp of annualised return — small, and reported for completeness rather than materiality, but it is the same shape as case study 2 and worth the client's attention as a pattern.

THE FIX — the highest value per hour of work in this report. Point the scorer at the traded venue's own trades, and correct the bar-stamp convention from open to close. Effort: roughly an hour. Do this before the other four fixes, because until it lands, every number you produce to measure those fixes is being scored against the wrong market. Separately, add a frozen-book check to the ingest — identical consecutive snapshots for longer than N seconds should raise, not flow through as a sustained extreme.

Reproduce: python3 audit/fc4_venue_reconcile.py --scored scored_calls.parquet --venue-trades trades/ --check-timestamp-convention

8. FC-8 — What is really being claimed?

What we asked. Is this a single number or a range? Is the error bar wide enough to include zero?

The claim as given contains no error bar at all. "+38.0% annualised, Sharpe 1.62" is quoted to one and two decimal places respectively, with no interval, no standard error and no sample size beside it. §3 has already shown what the number was doing — it was the maximum of 41 draws. This section asks what the corrected number can support.

Block bootstrap, 10,000 resamples, 7-day blocks to preserve autocorrelation, on the corrected series:

point estimate 95% CI
annualised return +2.2% −5.4% to +9.9%
Sharpe 0.14 −0.35 to 0.63
directional hit rate (holdout) 53.1% 50.2% to 56.0%

Read those three rows carefully, because they do not all say the same thing.

  • The return interval includes zero. On the evidence supplied, the corrected strategy cannot be distinguished from a strategy that makes nothing.
  • The Sharpe interval includes zero. Same statement, restated in risk-adjusted terms.
  • The hit-rate interval does not include chance, and is not close to it. 50.2% at the lower bound against a 33.3% floor. The directional information is established. The profitability is not.

That gap between the two is the actual state of this strategy, and it is a coherent, common position to be in: a signal that knows something, whose edge is smaller than the cost of acting on it under realistic execution.

The benchmark question, which the claim never asks. A three-way directional call has two lines to clear, not one. Chance is the floor. The naive alternative is the real bar — over 2023-01-01 to 2025-12-31 an always-long 1× position in the same 12 pairs, equally weighted, returned materially more than +2.2% annualised. The overlay is constructed to be broadly market-neutral, so it is not strictly the same trade, but no document in the material supplied states any benchmark, and a return claim with no comparison in it is not yet a claim about value. We flag this rather than resolving it: choosing the benchmark is the client's decision and it should be made in writing, in advance.

THE FIX — change what you publish, not what you compute. Quote a range with its sample size beside it, and state the benchmark. Effort: a decision, and an afternoon to restate the tear sheet. This is the cheapest credibility you will ever buy: an allocator who can check a number trusts it more than a larger one they cannot.

Reproduce: python3 fctools/fc8_bootstrap.py corrected_returns.parquet --block 672 --n 10000 --periods-per-year 35040 (672 fifteen-minute periods = 7-day blocks)

9. FC-2 — Are the dead ones missing?

What we asked. Was the asset list built as it looked then, or from what is still around today?

What we found. The 12 pairs are the top 12 by 30-day volume as measured in early 2026 — after the period under test. Two contracts that were in the top 12 for parts of 2023 have since been delisted and are absent from the backtest entirely. A third was listed mid-2024 and is included for the whole window, including nine months in which it did not exist to trade.
pairs in the backtest 12
pairs absent because they were later delisted 2
pair-months included in which the contract was not listed 9
effect of rebuilding the list as-of each date −1.8pp annualised

Small, and stated as small. This is the check that most often produces the largest finding — it does not here, because the universe is twelve liquid majors rather than a broad screen. We report the magnitude either way, because "we checked and it was minor" is a result the client paid for, and because it is the only way the reader can tell that the large findings elsewhere were sized rather than asserted.

One methodological note. We verified the as-of list against listing and delisting dates, not against the presence of price data — a contract can have rows in a panel long after it stops trading. That distinction is FC-2's second clause ("was any fix checked against the property it claims to test?") and it is where case study 3 came from.

THE FIX — build the list from listing events, not from what is trading today. Effort: about a day, and it is reusable across every strategy you run. Test it against the property it claims to hold, not against row counts: assert that no pair appears in the panel before its listing date or after its delisting date. A universe fix that is only checked by "the numbers changed" is how this defect survives a fix.

Reproduce: python3 audit/fc2_asof_universe.py --listings listing_events.csv --panel panel.parquet

10. What this report does not say

An audit that only reports damage is not worth paying for. Explicitly:

  • The model is not refuted. 53.1% directional accuracy against a 33.3% floor on untouched data, lower bound 50.2%, p < 0.001. There is information in this signal and none of the six corrections touches the model itself.
  • Nothing here is a coding error in the signal generator. Every finding is in the surrounding apparatus — the fill model, the clock, the cost model, the scoring series, the universe, the selection procedure. The signal code was the one part we found nothing in.
  • FC-1 passed cleanly, and that is a genuine credential. Most do not.
  • No dishonesty is alleged or implied anywhere in this report. Every defect found is one we have made ourselves; six of the ten case studies at arcobaleno.cloud are the same classes of error found in our own research and published against ourselves.
  • This is not an opinion on whether the strategy will make money in future. It is a statement about what the supplied material can and cannot support today. Several of the corrections are fixable — see §11 — and a fixed version is a different strategy that would need its own evidence.

11. Your remediation plan, in order

Every fix above, sequenced. Ranked by effect on the claim per unit of work, not by severity.

Read this as your plan, not our proposal. Each fix is specified precisely enough for your own engineers to implement without us — that is deliberate, and it is the shape the deliverable should take. We do not implement fixes on a strategy we have audited. The reason is not modesty: an auditor who also builds the correction has an interest in the size of the finding, and the only thing this report is worth anything for is that we do not. If you want the work done rather than specified, it is a separate engagement, taken by someone else, or taken by us only on the understanding that we can no longer be the ones who audit it.

And a boundary worth stating plainly: none of this tells you how to make the signal better. We have not suggested an indicator, a parameter or a feature, and we will not. Finding why a backtest lies is a different skill from knowing what to trade, and a firm that sells you both has an incentive to find problems it can also sell you the cure for. Everything above makes your measurement honest. Making the strategy better is your edge, and it should stay yours.

  1. Fix the scoring series before anything else (§7). It is a one-line source change plus a timestamp convention correction, it costs nothing, and until it is fixed every number the team produces — including the ones measuring the other fixes — is scored against the wrong market. Everything downstream depends on this being right first.
  2. Re-model fills as taker-by-default (§4), and treat maker fills as the exception requiring evidence. This inverts the current assumption. It is the honest default for a signal that trades on fast bars, and it converts the largest source of overstatement into a conservative one.
  3. Instrument snapshot age in production and feed the measured distribution back into the backtest (§5). This is the finding with the longest tail: it is the one that predicts the next live miss, and it cannot be fixed by changing the backtest alone — either the latency comes down or the signal must be told how stale its input is.
  4. Pre-register the next threshold change (§3). The selection problem cannot be fixed retrospectively — there is no way to un-see 41 results. It can only be prevented going forward, by fixing the bar before the window opens. This is a different engagement and we name it because it is the correct next step, not to sell it.
  5. Restate the tear sheet. The current investor-facing one-pager carries +38.0% with no interval. Whatever the corrected figure turns out to be after 1–3, it should be published as a range with a sample size beside it. A number an allocator can check is worth more than a larger number they cannot.

12. What we did not check

Stated so the boundary is legible, and not claimed on in either direction:

  • Anything after 2025-12-31. The material supplied ends there.
  • The live trading system's own code. We audited the research pipeline and instrumented the production logs for latency (§5). The order router, risk layer and reconciliation were out of scope.
  • Counterparty, custody, margin and liquidation mechanics.
  • Whether the venue data supplied is complete. We reconciled it against the client's own statements, which is a consistency check, not an independent verification.
  • Any pair outside the 12 supplied, and any horizon other than the 15-minute call.
